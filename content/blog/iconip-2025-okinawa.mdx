I returned to Japan in November 2025 for the International Conference on Neural Information Processing, or ICONIP. More than a year earlier, my first trip abroad had taken me to Yokohama. This time, the destination was Okinawa.

The itinerary covered November 16 to 23, eight days and seven nights. It began and ended at Naha Airport, with Onna as the base for the conference. The places between them made a much longer list.

## A different research question

The paper was [Factored Internal Verification Enhances Large Language Model Factuality](/publications#publication-factprompt), which introduced FactPrompt.

My earlier work had asked how instructions could help a model organize its reasoning. Here, I was concerned with checking facts. A fluent answer can still be wrong, and a model asked to review its own answer may repeat the mistake.

FactPrompt separates the initial answer, the generation of facts for checking it, and the revision. The point is to give the check some independence from the answer it is meant to assess.

That change in emphasis connects the two trips to Japan. In Yokohama, I brought work on getting a model to use what it knew. In Okinawa, I brought work on testing what it said.

## North and south from Onna

The city part of the plan began with Naminoue Shrine and Kokusai-dori in Naha. The following day's route headed north to Motobu, grouping the Okinawa Churaumi Aquarium, the Oceanic Culture Museum and its planetarium, and the Bise-Fukugi Tree Road.

The southern day had a different set of stops: Cape Chinen Park, Sefa-utaki, Ou Island and Gyokusendo. The itinerary put shrines, a cave and time by the water within the same day.

Elsewhere, Cape Zanpa and the Miyagi Coast appeared alongside a stop at Futenma Shrine. Another route paired the Nuchima-su salt factory and Kafu Banta with Katsuren Castle. Busena Marine Park and Kyoda were also on the list, before the final day's return toward Naha and Senaga Island.

These choices make the trip easier to recall as a whole. “Okinawa” was one destination on the flight booking; the itinerary spread it across towns, coasts, museums and historic sites.

## Time between places

The Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology also appeared in the route, connecting the university with the wider conference journey. Around it, the schedule made room for places with no academic purpose.

The transport estimates are a useful reminder of what planning involved. On paper, some bus journeys took two hours or more, and one estimate exceeded four. A long list of places needed a timetable as well as a map.

That is one reason I want to keep more than a list of attractions. The choices between them matter too: how far to go, what to put together, and how much of a day to leave for getting there.

Okinawa has its own place in these records now, alongside [the earlier journey to Yokohama and Mount Fuji](/blog/ijcnn-2024-yokohama-first-journey).
