Bremen was the reason for my August 2026 trip to Europe. The itinerary continued through Munich and Paris before ending in Berlin.

I went for the International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence, or IJCAI, where Adaptive GoGI-Skip had an oral presentation. The conference fixed the first destination. After it, the route had room for cities, museums, mountains and lakes.

> Bremen → Munich → Paris → Berlin

## A conference in Bremen

[Adaptive GoGI-Skip](/publications#publication-gogi-skip) asks how to reduce unnecessary work in a model's reasoning process. It combines a measure of each token's importance to the answer with the model's changing uncertainty.

The aim is to keep the parts that help and reduce redundancy. Accuracy matters, but so do the time and computation required to reach it. Those costs become harder to ignore as reasoning grows longer.

The Bremen itinerary put Congress Centrum Bremen alongside the town hall, Market Square, the Roland statue, the cathedral, the Schlachte waterfront and the Schnoor quarter. It gave the conference a place within the city, with time allocated to both.

There is a practical appeal to staying in one city for several days before moving on. The conference has its own timetable. The rest of the trip can take a different pace.

## Munich and the mountains

Munich was the next base. The city plan included Marienplatz and the New Town Hall, along with Asamkirche and stops at the Technical University of Munich and Ludwig Maximilian University.

The surrounding days were more spread out: Neuschwanstein Castle and Alpsee, then Zugspitze and Eibsee. These were destinations to give a day to, with travel time included, rather than names to squeeze between city stops.

I like seeing both kinds of day on the same itinerary. A square or a building can be part of a walk through a city. A mountain or a lake asks for a more deliberate choice about where to spend the day.

## Paris, Berlin and the return

The plan allowed four days for Paris, from August 25 to 28. It grouped the Marais, Sainte-Chapelle, Notre-Dame and the Seine with a longer list that included the Louvre, the Champs-Élysées, the Arc de Triomphe, the Eiffel Tower, Versailles and the Palais Garnier.

Writing them down makes the scale of the plan obvious. Museums, neighborhoods and major buildings need different amounts of time. Splitting the city across several days at least gave those interests separate places in the schedule.

Berlin was the last stop. The itinerary included the Brandenburg Gate, Potsdamer Platz, the Reichstag, Unter den Linden and sites associated with the Berlin Wall. It also set aside a day for Potsdam, with Sanssouci and the old town.

The route ended back in Germany before the flight home. IJCAI remains the academic entry for this journey. Bremen, Munich, Paris and Berlin give it a second, geographical account.
