I flew for the first time in the summer of 2024. I was going to Yokohama for the International Joint Conference on Neural Networks, or IJCNN. It was also my first trip abroad.

A paper gave me a reason to go. The trip grew to include Tokyo, Mount Tenran, Hakone, Kamakura, Mount Fuji, Mount Omuro and the cactus zoo in Izu. Looking back, I find it hard to separate the conference from the rest of that journey.

## A paper in Yokohama

The work I brought was [Unlocking Chain of Thought in Base Language Models by Heuristic Instruction](/publications#publication-heuristic-cot). It asked how clearer instructions could help a base language model use knowledge it already had.

Research usually reaches a publication list as a title, a date and a conference name. Going to Yokohama gave that entry a place. It also brought another task: explaining the work clearly enough for someone who had not spent months with it.

A paper has room for background, methods and experiments. A short explanation needs a direct route through them. I needed to explain the problem, the change I had made and what the experiments could establish. Preparing to discuss the work meant deciding which details carried the argument.

The practical preparations were new too. Alongside the paper were a visa, flights and an unfamiliar timetable. These were ordinary parts of travel, but I was doing them for the first time.

## Tokyo, towns and mountains

Tokyo was part of the trip, as were Mount Tenran, Hakone and Kamakura. Together, they gave me several ways to spend time outside the conference.

I like having that variety in a journey. A city, a town and a mountain make different demands on a day. They also leave room for interests that do not need a connection to my research.

Mount Omuro and the cactus zoo in Izu belong in this account for the same reason. They were places I went because I wanted to see them. An academic trip can have more than one purpose.

## Reaching the summit

I climbed Mount Fuji and reached the summit.

I want to remember the climb alongside the conference. They gave the trip two different goals, both worth the effort of getting there.

Later conferences took me to Vienna, Okinawa, Seoul and Bremen. The questions I brought changed, and flying became a familiar part of going somewhere. Yokohama remains where that began.

The next conference journey was [SMC 2025 in Vienna](/blog/smc-2025-vienna-europe).
