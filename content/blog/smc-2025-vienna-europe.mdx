Vienna appeared twice in my October 2025 itinerary: at the beginning, for a conference, and near the end, before the flight home. Between those two stays, the route took in Hallstatt, Rome and Florence.

The reason for going was the IEEE International Conference on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics, known as SMC. My paper, Twin Prompt, had an oral presentation.

> Vienna → Hallstatt → Rome and Florence → Vienna

The conference fixed the first part of the schedule. After it, I had room to arrange a different sort of day.

## Twin Prompt in Vienna

[Twin Prompt](/publications#publication-twin-prompt) concerns how language models understand a question and revise an answer. It asks the model to reformulate the question and refine its answer within one prompted response.

An oral presentation puts a limit on how much of a paper can be explained at once. I needed a clear account of the problem, the method and the evidence. Extra detail could help, but only after the audience knew why the method was worth considering.

The conference ran from October 5 to 8 at Austria Center Vienna. The city itinerary also listed Schönbrunn Palace, St. Stephen's Cathedral, the Kunsthistorisches Museum, the Vienna State Opera and the Musikverein.

Those choices show an interest that runs through the rest of the trip: I wanted to make time for museums, buildings and music, alongside the work that brought me there.

## Two days for Hallstatt

After the conference, I set aside two days for Hallstatt. The plan included viewpoints and walks around Waldbach Ursprung and Steinernes Herz.

I like where those days sit in the schedule. They come after several days organized around the conference and before the Italian part of the journey. Leaving time for walking gives the trip a change of pace.

It is tempting to treat the days between major destinations as gaps to fill. Here, they had a purpose of their own. Hallstatt had two days on the calendar rather than a brief stop on the way elsewhere.

## Rome, Florence and the return

The Italian part used Rome as a base, with a day planned for Florence. The final itinerary put Florence on October 12, followed by Rome's Colosseum and Palatine Hill, then the Vatican Museums.

The wider list included the Capitoline Museums and the Pantheon in Rome, and the Uffizi, the cathedral and Piazzale Michelangelo in Florence. It was an ambitious selection. Even on paper, it made clear how much time I wanted to give to art and history.

The route then returned to Vienna. Coming back to the same city gave the journey a simple shape: a conference at one end, time in the mountains and Italy in the middle, and a departure from Austria.

The research behind Twin Prompt is discussed in [Cognitive reasoning and cumulative intelligence](/blog/cognitive-reasoning-and-cumulative-intelligence). This account keeps the places around it.
